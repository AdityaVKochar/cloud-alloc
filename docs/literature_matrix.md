# Literature review matrix

This matrix records 13 foundational and closely related works. “Allocation outcomes” means the study evaluates downstream resource-management behavior, rather than reporting prediction error alone.

| Study | Data/workload | Main method or contribution | Allocation comparison/outcomes? | Relevance and gap retained |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Zhang, Hellerstein & Boutaba (2011), Characterizing Task Usage Shapes](https://research.google/pubs/characterizing-task-usage-shapes-in-google-compute-clusters/) | Google production task traces | Characterizes CPU, memory, and disk usage shapes | Indirect: synthetic-trace fidelity and utilization | Establishes stability of many tasks, supporting persistence as a serious baseline; not a predictive allocation system. |
| [Reiss et al. (2012), Heterogeneity and Dynamicity of Clouds at Scale](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2391229.2391236) | Google 2011 trace | Large-scale workload characterization | No direct predictive policy comparison | Motivates task-level variation and careful trace interpretation. |
| [Di, Kondo & Cirne (2012), Cloud versus Grid Workloads](https://research.google/pubs/pub42552) | Google production trace and grid traces | Compares job frequency, duration, and resource behavior | No | Supports use of real cloud traces and highlights their noisy, high-variance nature. |

## Gap synthesis

Prior work provides strong workload characterization, forecasting, learned scheduling, and production resource-management systems. However, studies often use private workloads, focus on one resource, report forecast accuracy without standardized downstream waste/deficit measures, or evaluate a single management policy. This project’s narrower contribution is a reproducible public-trace experiment that applies static, threshold-reactive, and predictive policies to identical task intervals and reports both forecast errors and CPU/memory allocation outcomes.
